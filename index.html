Moving to Türkiye



Why do people move to Türkiye?

There are roughly 3.6 million refugees living in Türkiye today. Around 2.3 million of these individuals have come to seek safety from the civil war in Syria, which has forced millions of people to leave their homes. Other people may leave their home countries to escape strict laws, conflict or political instability that can make everyday life difficult or unsafe. Türkiye is a common destination because of its geographical location between Europe, Asia and the Middle East, making it relatively accessible from several neighbouring countries. People may also choose Türkiye because of its paths to legal residency and the opportunities for greater freedom, security and safety it can offer. For many refugees and migrants, moving to Türkiye can therefore provide the chance to escape dangerous conditions and begin building a safer and more stable life.



Moving to a new country can bring many new opportunities and experiences, but it can also create serious challenges, especially for young people. Migrant children in Türkiye may have to adjust to a completely new environment while facing problems involving education, language barriers, child labour, discrimination, isolation and mental health. Some children may also be unaware of the services available to help them, making these problems even more difficult to overcome. These challenges can affect not only their everyday lives, but also their education, confidence and future. This website explores some of the most serious problems migrant children may face in Türkiye, in order of seriousness, along with possible solutions and practical advice that could help young migrants deal with these challenges.

1. Child Labor

Child labor stops children from doing and receiving necessities such as education and proper nutrition and care. This greatly affects their quality of life and their future. 19% of boys aged 12-14 and a whopping 48% of boys aged 15-17 are forced to work. Girls, however are much lower (5 and 9%).

2. Lack of education

Children, when not educated, can face serious problems in later life and may even get trapped in a loop of poverty. Being uneducated makes it harder to find well-paying jobs, which makes it harder for them to put their children in school, who get trapped in the same loop. Right now, only 48.57% of upper-secondary-age refugees go to school!

3. Unawareness of available services

If a child is unaware of the services they can be provided with, they may keep certain problems to themselves and may live with them for very long even if someone can help them. That is why making sure everyone is aware of the available services is very important.

4. Language barrier

Not knowing the language spoken (Turkish) can have a huge effect on the mental health of migrants. Their confidence may be greatly reduced, and they may be bullied at school. They also won't be able to communicate with people that can possibly help them.

5. Psychological Issues

Most migrants come after fleeing from war, so it is only natural that these refugees will have a high risk of developing mental health issues such as PTSD. However, this can completely change how the child thinks and behaves. Currently about 1 in every 4 Syrian refugee children aged 8–17 in an Istanbul study showed symptoms of depression, anxiety or PTSD.

6. Isolation

Isolation can be a serious problem for migrant children, especially when they have left their friends and family behind. Being in a new country where they may not know many people can make them feel lonely and left out. This can worsen other problems such as psychological issues and language barriers. In fact, 65% of Syrian refugee children in one Turkish study had high scores on a loneliness scale.

7. Discrimination

Discrimination can make it much harder for migrant children to feel accepted in a new country. They may be treated differently because of their nationality, language or background, which can greatly reduce their confidence and make them feel even more isolated. In fact, 44.1% of Syrian refugee adolescents reported experiencing bullying, compared with 27.8% of non-refugee adolescents..

Advice for young migrants

Moving to a new country can be difficult, but you don't have to face every problem alone. Try to learn basic Turkish so it is easier to communicate at school and in everyday life. Find out what services are available to you and ask a trusted adult, teacher or school for help if you don't know where to start. If you are struggling emotionally, talk to someone you trust instead of keeping it to yourself. Try to stay connected with friends and family and meet new people to avoid feeling isolated. Most importantly, speak up if you are being treated unfairly or feel unsafe.
