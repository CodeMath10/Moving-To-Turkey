Moving to Türkiye

Why do people move to Türkiye?

Many people move to Türkiye (formerly known as Turkey). In fact, there are roughly 3.6 million refugees living in Türkiye today. Around 2.3 million of these individuals have come to seek safety from the civil war in Syria. Other people leave their home countries to escape strict laws or political instability. They choose to move to Türkiye due to its geographical location, paths to legal residency, and the opportunities for freedom and safety it offers.



Moving to a new country can bring many opportunities, but it can also create serious challenges, especially for young people. Migrant children in Türkiye may face problems involving education, language, child labour, discrimination and mental health. This website explores some of the most serious problems they may face (in order of seriousness), possible solutions, and advice for young migrants.

1. Child Labor

Child labor stops children from doing and receiving necessities such as education and proper nutrition and care. This greatly affects their quality of life and their future. 19% of boys aged 12-14 and a whopping 48% of boys aged 15-17 are forced to work. Girls, however are much lower (5 and 9%).

2. Lack of education

When children grow up being uneducated, it usually means they find it very hard to get a well-paying job, and traps them in a loop of poverty. Only 48.57% of upper-secondary-age refugee/protected children attended upper secondary school.

3. Unawareness of available services

Not being aware of the services means if they are in a problem, even if the government can help them with ease, it may not help because the child never reached out to them

4. Language barrier

Even if children are going to school, not being able to speak the local language will greatly affect their confidence. They also may not be able to communicate with people who help what may be going on at home.

5. Psychological Issues

Psychological issues can completely change how the child thinks and behaves, making some people treat them differently and make them feel left out. Nearly 1 in 4 Syrian refugee children aged 8–17 in an Istanbul study showed symptoms of depression, anxiety or PTSD.

6. Isolation

Isolation only worsens the effects of everything above. They stop sharing their thoughts and problems with family and feel like they have no one to talk to. 65% of Syrian refugee children in one Turkish study had high scores on a loneliness scale.

7. Discrimination

Like isolation, discrimination will only worsen the issues and make it even less likely the child will make friends. 44.1% of Syrian refugee adolescents reported experiencing bullying, compared with 27.8% of non-refugee adolescents. It will also greatly reduce the child’s confidence and make them feel all the more isolated.

Advice for young migrants

Moving to a new country can be difficult, but you don't have to face every problem alone. Try to learn basic Turkish so it is easier to communicate at school and in everyday life. Find out what services are available to you and ask a trusted adult, teacher or school for help if you don't know where to start. If you are struggling emotionally, talk to someone you trust instead of keeping it to yourself. Try to stay connected with friends and family and meet new people to avoid feeling isolated. Most importantly, speak up if you are being treated unfairly or feel unsafe.
